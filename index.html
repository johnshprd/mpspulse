The Science

Your body can't store
unused protein.

Muscle protein synthesis (MPS) has a ceiling. Once triggered — typically by ~3–4g of leucine — it runs for around 2–3 hours and then shuts off, regardless of how much more protein you eat. The excess is simply oxidised for energy.

TYPICAL ATHLETE — FRONT-LOADED
⚠ 1× MPS window — most protein wasted
PULSE ATHLETE — EVENLY SPREAD
✓ 4× MPS windows — protein fully utilised
01
MPS TRIGGERS PER MEAL
Each meal containing ~3–4g leucine (roughly 30–40g protein) triggers a full synthesis response lasting 2–3 hours.
02
THE CEILING EFFECT
Eating 80g in one sitting doesn't give you 2× the benefit. Beyond ~40–55g, additional protein is oxidised — not used for muscle repair or growth.
03
THE PULSE STRATEGY
Spacing 3–4 protein pulses of 30–50g across the day maximises total synthesis time and ensures every gram works harder.

Questions

Straight answers.

IS THE MPS SCIENCE LEGIT? ▼
Yes. The protein pulse model is based on peer-reviewed research, including work by Stuart Phillips (McMaster University) and Luc van Loon (Maastricht University) — two of the world's leading experts in protein metabolism and muscle physiology.
DOES IT WORK IF I EAT 3 MEALS? ▼
Absolutely. 3 evenly-spaced pulses still significantly outperforms loading most of your protein into one meal. PULSE will show your distribution score and flag if you'd benefit from a 4th pulse like a pre-bed snack (e.g. casein or cottage cheese).
HOW IS THIS DIFFERENT FROM MYFITNESSPAL? ▼
MyFitnessPal tracks totals. PULSE tracks timing and distribution — the part that actually determines how much of your protein gets used for muscle. Most athletes are already eating enough protein. Almost none are eating it at the right times.
