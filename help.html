THE BASICS
What makes MPS Pulse different from other protein trackers? ▼
Most apps count your daily total — but research shows that when you eat protein matters just as much as how much you eat. MPS Pulse tracks your protein across 4 evenly-spaced meal windows (pulses) throughout the day, because this pattern maximises the time your muscles spend in a growth state.
What is Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS)? ▼
MPS is the process your body uses to build and repair muscle tissue. Every time you eat a meaningful amount of protein, your body triggers an MPS window — a period of active muscle-building that lasts roughly 2–3 hours. After that window closes, eating more protein from the same meal has diminishing returns. The key insight: 4 separate triggers across the day beats one large protein meal.

How do I set my protein goal? ▼
Tap the GOAL pill in the header or go to Settings → Daily Protein Goal. The evidence-based recommendation is 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of bodyweight per day. For example, if you weigh 80kg, aim for 128–176g. Your 4 pulse targets will automatically distribute evenly from your goal.
PULSES & TIMING
How far apart should my pulses be? ▼
Aim for 3–4 hours between pulses. This gives each MPS window time to run its course before the next one is triggered. Eating protein too soon after a previous pulse doesn't open a new window — it just adds to one that's already winding down.
⚠ The app will warn you if two pulses are less than 3 hours apart.
Do I have to eat exactly at Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner and Evening? ▼
No — those are just labels. What matters is that you have 4 protein meals spaced roughly 3–4 hours apart across your waking day. You can eat at any times that suit your schedule. Log each meal into whichever pulse slot is next in your day.
What if I miss a pulse? ▼
Missing a pulse means missing a potential MPS window for that period — but it doesn't ruin the day. Just hit your remaining pulses as best you can. The app will acknowledge missed windows without nagging you about something you can't fix. Tomorrow is a fresh set of 4.
Can I change the time on a pulse? ▼
Yes — once you've logged at least one food in a pulse, a time field appears next to the MPS badge. Tap it to edit the pulse time. This affects the MPS countdown, so use it if you forgot to log a meal at the time you actually ate it.
MPS WINDOWS
What does the dashed bar in each pulse card mean? ▼
The dashed MPS bar shows how far through the current MPS window you are. It starts empty when you log your first food in a pulse, then fills over the next 3 hours as the window progresses. When it's full and shows ENDED, that MPS window has closed.
What does ACTIVE, ENDING, and ENDED mean? ▼
ACTIVE — your MPS window is open and your muscles are in a building state.

ENDING — the window is in its final stretch (last ~1 hour). You can still add to this pulse but the window is winding down.

ENDED — the window has closed. Adding more food to this pulse won't restart it.
Why is there a 55g ceiling? ▼
Research suggests your muscles can only utilise around 40–55g of protein per meal for MPS. Eating more than this in a single pulse doesn't stack up — the excess gets used for energy or excreted rather than going toward muscle growth. The app warns you when a pulse exceeds this ceiling.
DISTRIBUTION SCORE
What is the distribution score? ▼
The distribution score (0–100) measures how evenly spread your protein is across your 4 pulses. A score of 100 means all 4 pulses were identical in size — perfect distribution. Lower scores mean your protein was uneven, with some pulses much larger than others.
85+ = Optimal · 60–84 = Good · 35–59 = Uneven · Below 35 = Poor
Why does the score say IN PROGRESS early in the day? ▼
The score is contextual — it doesn't penalise you for having empty pulses when the day isn't over yet. Early in the day it shows IN PROGRESS rather than a low score, since you haven't had the chance to fill all 4 pulses yet. The full verdict only appears once all 4 pulses have been logged.
LOGGING FOOD
How do I add a food that isn't in the database? ▼
Type the food name in the search box — if it doesn't match anything, an "Add to My Foods" button will appear. Tap it to open the custom food modal. Enter the food name, emoji, typical serving size in grams, and how much protein is in that serving. It'll be saved to your personal food library.
What does the 📸 SCAN button do? ▼
The scan button uses AI to analyse a photo of your meal and estimate the protein content of each food it detects. Take or upload a photo, tap Analyse, then review the detected foods and their protein estimates. You can tick or untick individual items before logging them to your pulse.
I added a food retrospectively — what happens to the pulse time? ▼
If you add food to a pulse within 5 minutes of your first entry, it logs silently using the original time — no prompt. If it's been more than 5 minutes, you'll be asked whether to keep the original pulse time or update it to now. This matters because the MPS countdown is based on the pulse time.
Why does the serving size default to 100g? ▼
It shouldn't — the app uses each food's typical serving size as the default (e.g. 180g for a chicken breast, 200g for a pot of Greek yoghurt). If you're seeing 100g, the food may have been added with a corrupted serving history. Open Settings → Manage Foods, tap the edit icon next to the food, and set the correct typical serving size.
ACCOUNT & SETTINGS
Does my data reset every day? ▼
Yes — your pulse log resets at midnight each day. Your previous day's total is saved to the 7-day history automatically before resetting, so you can track your consistency over the week.
How do I reset today's log? ▼
Go to Settings (⚙️) → Reset Today. This clears all of today's entries across all 4 pulses. This action can't be undone.
How do I manage or delete foods from my library? ▼
Go to Settings → Manage Foods. You can edit any food (including built-in ones) with the ✏️ button, or remove foods from the database with 🗑. Removed built-in foods can be restored with the restore button at the bottom of the list.
Is my data backed up? ▼
Yes — your data is saved to your account in real time. As long as you're signed in, your food logs, history, custom foods, and settings are all synced and will be there if you switch devices or reinstall the app.
